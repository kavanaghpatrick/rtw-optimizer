# oneworld Explorer Booking Edge Cases — Unofficial Community Knowledge

> **Status**: Community-sourced, UNOFFICIAL knowledge layer. Everything here comes from FlyerTalk, Australian Frequent Flyer (AFF), and Reddit field reports — not from the fare rules. It assumes full fluency with official IATA Rule 3015 (edition 27 FEB 2026); nothing in the official rules is repeated here except where desks misapply it.
>
> **Research date**: 2026-06-10. 144 raw findings deduped into the entries below. 43 high-impact claims went through adversarial verification (36 confirmed, 6 plausible, 1 contradicted); the rest are tagged from researcher confidence.
>

> |-----|---------|

## Part 1 — Cross-cutting tactics

### 1.1 Married-segment games

**Marriage diagnosis discipline — triangulate before acting** `[community-consensus]`
The standard signature (per-leg availability that vanishes on the through-search) has false positives (not-yet-launched routes look married — [henry999's AKL-PER case, Aug 2025](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2008084-oneworld-explorer-user-guide-71.html)) and hidden axes (POS silently filters everything; aa.com desktop/mobile/agent tools show different inventory for the same O&D — [Dec 2018](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/american-airlines-aadvantage/1885940-more-award-availability-restricted-married-segments-connections-20.html)). Before invoking any workaround: (1) EF through-journey AND per-leg, (2) repeat under the itinerary's actual POS, (3) operating carrier's own website, (4) confirm the route operates that date. Only when per-leg space survives all four and through-space doesn't is marriage confirmed ([User Guide p42, Nov 2024](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2008084-oneworld-explorer-user-guide-42-printerfriendly.html)).

**Hub O&D control: standalone D0, connecting D open (and vice versa) — always query both ways** `[confirmed]`
CX (HKG) and QR (DOH) run O&D revenue management: the same flight shows D0 standalone but D available as a through-hub connection — or the reverse (littlevoices' HKG-DOH-MAN: EF showed D7/D4 per leg, agent saw nothing until the married pair was recreated as a connection, revealing only D2 truly existed). For any segment touching a hub: run BOTH the standalone leg and the full O&D connection in EF's connection search before calling, and feed the agent the exact married combination (both flight numbers, one transaction) rather than asking for legs individually. Verified against CX's own published O&D booking policy. ([FT 2152207 p2, Jan 2025](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2152207-aa-rtw-desk-availability-compared-expertflyer-ow-online-booking-tool-2.html); [Booking & Pricing p182, 2025](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/1776577-oneworld-booking-pricing-experiences-182.html); [CX agents O&D policy PDF, eff. Sep 2023](https://www.cxagents.com/content/dam/cathay-agents/us/documents/agents-resources/CX_Abusive_Booking_Policy_eff_01Sep23v2_cxagents.pdf))

**Codeshare flight numbers draw on separate, unmarried inventory — the cleanest bypass** `[corroborated-thin]`
Marriage attaches to the operating carrier's flight number; the codeshare marketing code on the same metal is inventoried independently and is frequently open when the prime code is married shut. Firsthand wins 12 years apart: AA7973 on QR DME-DOH-JFK metal ([Nov 2013](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/338667-oneworld-explorer-ticket-faqs-41-printerfriendly.html)); FJ 5252 on QF SYD-PER when BA's desk saw no QF D ([Aug 2025](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2008084-oneworld-explorer-user-guide-71.html)); AA-coded DFW-DOH + IB-coded DOH-AGP on QR metal when QR prime had no D ([Booking & Pricing p186, 2025](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/1776577-oneworld-booking-pricing-experiences-186.html)). Action: list every codeshare on the flight in EF, check each marketing carrier's bucket. Traps: the oneworld tool will not show codeshares (phone only); some desks treat a code swap as a chargeable reroute (BA claimed a fee, AA did not); codeshare choice changes YQ; CX cannot carry AA's code transpacific (Russia overflight).

**Build 24h+ stopovers, not connections — but know the inverse trap** `[confirmed]`
Most carriers only marry connections under 24h (the claimed 4-hour auto-marry threshold is AA-domestic-specific). Veterans deliberately structure 24h+ gaps so segments sell unmarried, preserving change flexibility — you can convert a stopover into a connection later but never the reverse. INVERSE TRAP: D sometimes exists ONLY as the married pair and not point-to-point (seen "frequently" on AA and QF), so an unmarried build can show strictly less availability — check EF both ways and accept that whichever way you book is the way you're stuck searching at change time. ([dvs7310, User Guide p42, Oct 2024](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2008084-oneworld-explorer-user-guide-42-printerfriendly.html); [outsmart-AA thread, 2011](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/american-airlines-aadvantage-pre-consolidation-usair/1174719-how-outsmart-aa-beat-married-segments-3.html))

**Post-ticketing changes re-run the FULL O&D availability check** `[confirmed]`
Changing one flight of a married pair requires the entire new O&D to re-show the booked class — including the unchanged, already-held segment. Documented system-blocks on AA (2018), QF (2023, "agents could see space but the computer wouldn't let them"), QR (2025). Cancelling may not return seats to inventory, so cancel-and-rebook is unsafe. One logic exception (Dave Noble): DROPPING a connection (A-B-C-D → A-C-D keeping held C-D) can work; substituting a leg cannot. If a schedule change breaks a marriage, refuse any "rebook whole O&D in higher class at your cost" demand — the correct handling is involuntary reissue with a waiver code, no additional collection (QR once tried to extract ~USD 755/pax this way). ([AA thread p20, Dec 2018](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/american-airlines-aadvantage/1885940-more-award-availability-restricted-married-segments-connections-20.html); [AFF QF MSL thread, Feb 2023](https://www.australianfrequentflyer.com.au/community/threads/article-qantas%E2%80%99-frustrating-application-of-married-segment-logic.108155/); [QR Avios thread, Feb 2025](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/qatar-airways-privilege-club/2160012-avios-redemption-married-segment-ways-around.html))

**PNR context poisons availability — have the agent strip <24h conflicting segments before searching** `[multiple-reports]`
On changes to an existing PNR, the GDS evaluates new segments against segments already in the record; flights within 24h of the target can marry against it and suppress D that EF shows standalone. Procedure (ernestnywang): agent removes the conflicting segments from the working PNR without ending the transaction, re-runs availability, re-adds. Agents do not do this by default — you must ask. ([EF-going-downhill thread, Dec 2024](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2180862-expertflyer-going-downhill-am-i-doing-something-wrong.html))

**Never ask an agent to "break the marriage" — ADM risk is real and quantified** `[community-consensus]`
Airlines publish "Breaking of Married Segments" as a named agency violation; LOT's published fine is USD 600 per PNR per passenger plus fare differential ([Sabre Taiwan policy PDF, 2019](https://sabretn.com.tw/admin/fileupload/132194099812834769191128LO.pdf)), and agent-side posters confirm manual force-sells are ADM-able/fireable. Breaking IS occasionally done — the AA RTW desk "solved it by breaking the married segment" on a CX CTS-HKG block ([Feb 2025](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/1776577-oneworld-booking-pricing-experiences-182.html)) — but methods are carrier-specific, guarded, and inconsistent (Amadeus XE only works on pairs not truly married; Sabre returns "Carrier refuses cancellation"). Phrase it as "can you work the combination?" or call the OPERATING carrier with a valid reason; let a specialist TA choose their own method — they carry the ADM risk. ([Amadeus XE thread, 2017-2023](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/online-travel-booking-bidding-agencies/1885075-amadeus-breaking-married-segments-cant-broken-using-xe.html))

**Desk choice determines marriage exposure: segment-by-segment sellers sidestep it** `[multiple-reports]`
The AA RTW desk dictates itineraries flight-by-flight, which largely sidesteps O&D marriage at booking time; GDS-bound TAs (Galileo etc.) must request stopover-to-stopover O&Ds, which is exactly where marriage bites. When using a TA, ask whether they can long-sell individual flight numbers. Residual risk: a build that books into married-only inventory is married for all later changes. ([User Guide p36, Sep 2024](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2008084-oneworld-explorer-user-guide-36.html))

**Married-pair sells need a single transaction and agent patience — HUCA legitimately changes outcomes** `[multiple-reports]`
On contested CX/MH pairs, agents need 20-30 minutes of "dancing" through sell orders and unconfirmed (UC) responses before the marriage confirms; one agent sees nothing where the next sees the legs. Spoon-feed the exact construction, ask for different sell order, and hang-up-call-again before concluding it's unbookable. ([Booking & Pricing p182, 2025](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/1776577-oneworld-booking-pricing-experiences-182.html); [FT 2152207, 2024](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2152207-aa-rtw-desk-availability-compared-expertflyer-ow-online-booking-tool.html))

**Last-resort: deliberate no-show at the connection point forces a $125 rebooking against standalone inventory** `[single-source]`
Proposed (not deliberately tested) by dvs7310 from a real accidental precedent. Only viable with next-day standalone availability verified, no checked bags, and acceptance that a mishandled no-show can cascade-cancel downline coupons — coordinate with the desk immediately after missing the flight. ([User Guide p36, Sep 2024](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2008084-oneworld-explorer-user-guide-36.html))

### 1.2 Post-ticketing change games

**Fly the first coupon ASAP — pre-departure touches to segment 1 or ticketed points reprice the whole fare** `[confirmed]`
Before the first coupon is lifted, any change touching segment 1 (even date-only) or any ticketed-point change triggers a reprice at current fare levels if the fare rose. After segment 1 is flown the base fare is permanently locked. Never put a dummy date on segment 1; engineer it as a short, cheap, D-rich hop (community standard: AY OSL-HEL) and fly it as soon as practical. Verified against the 27 FEB 2026 Rule 3015 filing text plus multiple firsthand reports. ([User Guide wiki + p38/p45-46](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2008084-oneworld-explorer-user-guide-46.html); [Rule 3015 27 FEB 26 PDF](https://assets.ctfassets.net/m9ph4qvas97u/58dSxVDQ0kjLFD2Dsxpo6m/0ae0e100a274267777529778cbe91473/oneworld_Explorer_27_FEB_26.pdf))

**Dummy dates everywhere else — the canonical answer to D0 on your real dates** `[confirmed]`
Date/time and same-route carrier/flight changes are free and unlimited (D permitting), so book contested segments on ANY date showing D and roll forward later. Wrinkles: the placeholder itself needs D (can't book D0 even as a dummy); changes must keep chronological order (cascade shuffles); a segment left in L "to fix later" is trapped if the fare rises — reissuing it into D reprices the whole ticket, so find ANY D date instead. Bundle date changes — six free changes in one call is documented (Tetrarch, Feb 2026). Open-dating is rule-legal but every desk refuses it in practice (AA, BA, CX all declined; AA's desk has occasionally issued open segments when the rule is quoted — don't rely on it). ([User Guide p46, Dec 2024](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2008084-oneworld-explorer-user-guide-46.html); [ExpertFlyer vs BA, Feb 2026](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2213364-expertflyer-vs-ba.html); [User Guide p40, Oct 2024](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2008084-oneworld-explorer-user-guide-40.html))

**The $125 is per TRANSACTION — one call can rebuild the entire remaining itinerary** `[community-consensus]`
Post-departure, ticketed-point changes cost USD 125 for everything done in the same transaction: a user replaced 13 remaining segments in one change for one $125; a professional ticketer confirmed 4 flights = one fee. Plan tickets knowing the routing you buy is not the routing you must fly — what matters at purchase is a valid, D-available skeleton plus a flyable first segment. Hold every desired routing edit until they can execute together. If quoted $125 per segment, that is wrong — quote Rule 16. ([User Guide p38, Sep 2024](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2008084-oneworld-explorer-user-guide-38.html); [Terrible fare thread, Jun 2024](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2155530-terrible-fare-changing-rtw-ticket-issued-qf.html))

**The $125 doubles as a D-availability escape hatch** `[multiple-reports]`
When D is gone on your wanted routing at change time, changing the ticketed points themselves (different connection city, split surface) re-shops the whole remaining routing against live D inventory for one fee — felix1224 converted EZE-MIA-YYZ to EZE-JFK//LGA-YYZ specifically because no D existed via MIA. Pre-shop the alternative in EF and present it; don't ask the agent to search. ([RTW-through-Qantas thread, May 2024](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2161590-rtw-ticket-through-qantas-but-they-charging-changing-fee.html))

**The 24-hour stopover/transit boundary is a silent fee/reprice tripwire** `[community-consensus]`
A "free" date change that pushes a connection across the 24h line is a ticketed-point change: $125, possibly a reprice, and some airlines/agents treat the stopover↔transit flip as a full reroute while others process it free. Recompute every affected connection against the 24h line before requesting any date change; if a move must cross it, bundle it with other routing changes under one $125. Ask neutrally — never volunteer that it's a stopover conversion. ([User Guide p45, Dec 2024](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2008084-oneworld-explorer-user-guide-45.html); [AFF skylabsea DONE4](https://www.australianfrequentflyer.com.au/community/threads/my-done4-oneworld-rtw-booking-experience.111025/))

**Involuntary schedule changes are free-change currency — including reroutes onto carriers not on the ticket** `[community-consensus]`
Any cancellation or material schedule change waives the $125 and fare difference and can buy routings unbookable voluntarily: a BA AMM-LHR cancellation was converted by the AA desk into AMM-xDOH-SEA on QR Qsuites at zero cost; AA waived a route-change fee because QR had retimed an unrelated first flight by 7 hours. Log every schedule-change email however minor; when you need a change the desk won't do free, ask for it to be processed as involuntary alongside a pending schedule change. Never accept the auto-rebook — propose your preferred routing with D verified. ([AFF skylabsea, Jan 2024](https://www.australianfrequentflyer.com.au/community/threads/my-done4-oneworld-rtw-booking-experience.111025/); [User Guide p40, Oct 2024](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2008084-oneworld-explorer-user-guide-40.html))

**A leg ticketed in economy on a DONEx can be restored to business free when D opens** `[multiple-reports]`
The fare basis still entitles you to business; set an EF alert on D for the exact flight (or whole married O&D — married carriers may require D across the pair) and call the plater the moment it fires. Carriers that release only J/C close-in do you no good — the RBD must be D. CX has even reissued NO-ADC into D post-ticketing. ([Downgraded-one-flight thread, Aug 2024](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2170176-rtw-oneworld-downgraded-one-flight-possible-re-upgrade.html))

**Dropping the final coupon is tolerated in practice — zero enforcement in 13+ years** `[community-consensus]`
The repricing right for incomplete journeys has never been reported enforced (Dr. HFH, restated 2012→2025), including on the heavily-gamed ex-CAI cohort. Route your real endpoint as the penultimate stop and no-show the cheap return-to-origin leg: carry-on only on the penultimate leg, expect no refund beyond unused airport taxes. NEVER no-show a mid-ticket coupon — downline coupons cascade-cancel. Formal alternative: remove a ticketed point for $125. ([Point-of-origin thread, 2012](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/1417740-point-origin-return-question.html); [Cancellation-refund thread, Jan 2025](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2183904-rtw-ticket-cancellation-refund.html))

**Partial-use cancellation yields ~zero refund — convert, don't cancel** `[community-consensus]`
Flown segments revalue at point-to-point fares that exceed the RTW fare almost immediately; at best you recover unused airport taxes minus a fee (8-12 weeks). If plans collapse mid-trip: free-date-change the remainder to ~11 months out to keep the ticket alive while deciding, then redirect remaining coupons somewhere useful in one $125 transaction. Never let an agent "helpfully" cancel coupons. ([Cancellation-refund thread, Jan 2025](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2183904-rtw-ticket-cancellation-refund.html))

**Cheap-origin (devalued-POC) tickets attract hostile change handling** `[confirmed]`
Origin pricing is real and decoupled from point of sale (you can buy an ex-OSL fare through the Dallas desk), but aggressively cheap origins draw servicing hostility for the ticket's life: ex-CAI holders were quoted AUD 3,733-10,065 for $125 changes, told fares were "greatly undervalued," and stonewalled with inapplicable rule sections (16(a)3 mileage recalc, which applies to GLOB not DONEx). Ex-OSL is currently the "safe" cheap origin; ex-CAI is the cautionary tale; ex-Japan is suspected next. Defenses: fly segment 1 immediately, keep changes free-tier, demand the agent actually run any claimed recalculation, escalate in writing. ([AFF RTW guide, Apr 2025](https://www.australianfrequentflyer.com.au/oneworld-explorer-rtw-guide/); [User Guide p40, Oct 2024](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2008084-oneworld-explorer-user-guide-40.html); [Booking & Pricing p120, Mar 2024](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/1776577-oneworld-booking-pricing-experiences-120.html))

### 1.3 D-class availability games

**Phantom D: ExpertFlyer is a leg-level cached ceiling, never confirmation** `[confirmed]`
EF reads cached leg-level availability; the agent's sell is additionally filtered by POS, point of commencement, O&D bid-price logic, married-segment control, agency-level blocks, and the Rule 3015 capacity clause. Failures cluster on CX, QR, JL, QF; AA domestic is most reliable. Sometimes the block is correct forecasting (a CX flight blocked at EF-D7 organically sold down to D0/J0). Score segments: D7-D9 on a generous carrier 90+ days out = likely bookable; D1-D2, hub carrier, or <30 days = expect failure. Have 2-3 alternates ready for every hub segment before any call. (The often-quoted ~70-85% EF-to-desk match rate is an editorial estimate, not sourced data.) ([FT 2152207](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2152207-aa-rtw-desk-availability-compared-expertflyer-ow-online-booking-tool.html); [EF-going-downhill, Dec 2024](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2180862-expertflyer-going-downhill-am-i-doing-something-wrong.html); [Booking & Pricing p182, Feb 2025](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/1776577-oneworld-booking-pricing-experiences-182.html))

**Point of Commencement is the dominant hidden filter; flipping EF POS is a red herring** `[confirmed]`
Carriers increasingly filter Explorer D/A by where the whole ticket commences. EF only exposes POS — identical D7 across UK/Norway/India POS while the desk sees D0 is the signature (Tetrarch, Feb 2026, AA1080 SJO-DFW). The block is not desk-specific: the same flights stay blocked whether AA or JL is the booking carrier (anabolism, Apr 2026). Only an Amadeus terminal can query by POC. Don't burn time cycling EF POS settings; price the POC penalty into origin selection (the restriction follows the ticket onto every later change); verify critical segments via a dummy xONEx build from the ACTUAL origin or via an Amadeus TA. ([ExpertFlyer vs BA, Feb 2026](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2213364-expertflyer-vs-ba.html); [FT 2152207 p2, Apr 2026](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2152207-aa-rtw-desk-availability-compared-expertflyer-ow-online-booking-tool-2.html))

**The Sabre 330-day wall — and the D-not-yet-loaded trap at the schedule edge** `[confirmed]`
AA/AS/WY sit on Sabre (~330-331 day horizon) vs ~355-361 on Amadeus carriers; the AA desk literally cannot look at dates beyond it. Don't switch ticketing carrier just to reach far dates — book placeholders inside the window and roll forward free. When dates first roll into the window, D is often not yet loaded: a freshly-opened date showing D0 is not a verdict; re-scan a week or two later. ([ExpertFlyer vs BA, Feb 2026](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2213364-expertflyer-vs-ba.html); [User Guide wiki](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2008084-oneworld-explorer-user-guide.html))

**Close-in volatility: blocks lift randomly, equipment swaps open D, and space also evaporates** `[multiple-reports]`
AA transcon blocks have lifted ~8 days out ("quite random" per the agent); an A330→777 swap opened "loads of availability" matching EF; conversely JL A-space has been whittled to zero pre-flight. No dependable T-x release calendar exists for any carrier. Set EF alerts on D/A AND equipment type; re-attempt after any schedule/equipment-change notice; schedule deliberate retry passes at ~14 and ~8 days; never sit on unticketed close-in D — confirm same-call. BA sales trigger transient wholesale D flashes — act within hours. ([FT 2152207, Sep 2024](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2152207-aa-rtw-desk-availability-compared-expertflyer-ow-online-booking-tool.html); [Booking & Pricing p182, Feb 2025](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/1776577-oneworld-booking-pricing-experiences-182.html))

**Agent-initiated escalations exist but you must name them: one-off space-open and partner liaisons** `[corroborated-thin]`
Per Dr. HFH: the AA desk has "a procedure by which there's someone they can ask to open the space for you, on a one-off basis," and AA staffs human liaisons to each partner carrier ("ask the agent to raise it with the JL liaison for that flight"). Passengers who don't name the mechanism get a flat no. Both are agent-dependent — HUCA. ([FT 2152207, Sep 2024 / Jan 2025](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2152207-aa-rtw-desk-availability-compared-expertflyer-ow-online-booking-tool-2.html))

**Waitlists: accept when offered, expect never to clear** `[single-source]`
The AA desk offers waitlists on space it can see but not sell. No poster has ever reported an RTW waitlist clearing into confirmed D/A. Pair every waitlist with a confirmed dummy-date booking on an alternate. ([FT 2152207, May 2024](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2152207-aa-rtw-desk-availability-compared-expertflyer-ow-online-booking-tool.html))

**B (non-AA) / H (AA) economy fallback keeps an unbookable itinerary alive — desks and scanners ignore it** `[community-consensus]`
Rule 3015 lets DONE passengers book L when D is gone, and B (H on AA) when L is also gone — the segment flies in economy but the ticket remains constructible, and you switch back into D later via free change. Agents frequently don't know this; quote the rule note. Scanner implication: alert D first, then L, then B/H as the keep-alive tier. (Note: AA's business RBD is D like everyone else; H is AA's fallback. **RESOLVED 2026-06**: carriers.yaml now sets AA primary=D with `rtw_fallback_class: H` (B for other carriers), and `rtw verify` re-scans the fallback class on a D0 result via `get_fallback_class()`.) ([Rule 3015 27 FEB 26 PDF](https://assets.ctfassets.net/m9ph4qvas97u/58dSxVDQ0kjLFD2Dsxpo6m/0ae0e100a274267777529778cbe91473/oneworld_Explorer_27_FEB_26.pdf))

**Healthy-D trunk profile: carrier release policy beats frequency** `[corroborated-thin]`
BA/QR/CX/AY/JL wide-body trunks scan dependably open (QR deepest J ladder); the D0 profile has two classes — long-thin weekly routes (FJ SIN-NAN, QF JNB-SYD, ex-HA HNL-PPT) and stingy/batch carriers where even 12x-daily stays D0 (QF, AS; UL sporadic batches). Anchor contested continent-crossings on the dependable five; treat any weekly-frequency or QF/AS/UL segment as D0-until-proven with a pre-verified alternate; re-verify QF/AS/UL same-day before any call. (Verification corroborated the hierarchy but flagged specifics as volatile — see Part 4.) ([QF-drop-D thread, Feb 2026](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2212214-qantas-drop-d-class-mel-syd-dps.html); [FT 2152207 p2, Mar 2025](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2152207-aa-rtw-desk-availability-compared-expertflyer-ow-online-booking-tool-2.html))

### 1.4 Surface and ARNK tactics

**The origin open jaw burns no coupon — 16 flights + origin OJ tickets fine** `[confirmed]`
A Rule 4(c) origin/turnaround open jaw is NOT entered as an ARNK and does not consume a coupon; 16 flown segments plus the origin OJ is ticketable (multiple firsthand tickets, one BA-issued). Agents sometimes invent the opposite — push back: no ARUNK is inserted between the last coupon's destination and the first coupon's origin, or HUCA/reissue elsewhere. ([serfty, User Guide post #1221, Jan 2026](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/37558959-post1221.html); [OW Explorer Question thread, Feb 2018](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/1892230-oneworld-explorer-question.html))

**Mid-itinerary surfaces consume a coupon; 16 is an IATA e-ticket conjunction limit no desk can waive** `[confirmed]`
Every open jaw between flight coupons gets an ARUNK coupon; 4 coupons x 4 conjuncted tickets = 16, hard. Never lobby for a 17th — technically impossible. Forced over-limit reroutes mean losing something: AA's old delete-and-restore workaround is dead (~2021-23, "no more than 16 segments, period"); the clean fix — exchanging remaining segments onto a NEW ticket, which resets the limit (PNRs support multiple e-tickets) — is technically possible but airline-willingness-gated; ask explicitly. Leave a coupon of slack on maxed builds relying on thin routes. ([OW Explorer Question, 2018](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/1892230-oneworld-explorer-question.html); [FAQ p63, 2014](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/338667-oneworld-explorer-ticket-faqs-63.html); [IRROPS thread, Nov 2024](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2178436-irrops-rtw-tickets.html))

**Same-city airport changes (HND//NRT, LHR//LCY, JFK//LGA) consume a full coupon — even regulars get this wrong** `[community-consensus]`
Any co-terminal arrival/departure mismatch is one full segment toward 16 regardless of stay length. Route to depart the airport you arrived at. ([AFF same-city thread, Jan 2024](https://www.australianfrequentflyer.com.au/community/threads/oneworld-rtw-products-same-city-surface-sectors.112284/))

**Eliminate-the-surface trick: reroute via a connecting hub, bank the coupon** `[community-consensus]`
Short forced surfaces (LGA//JFK, LHR//LGW, CDG//ORY) can be engineered away with a one-stop routing into the same airport — each surface eliminated = one extra flown segment or stopover elsewhere. Corollary: replace short intra-Europe/SE-Asia flights with trains/LCCs to bank coupons for long-haul. ([User Guide wiki + p4](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2008084-oneworld-explorer-user-guide-4.html))

**Surface past D-droughts and network gaps; nest separate tickets when coupons are tight** `[confirmed]`
Standard practice: surface any pair with no D or no oneworld carrier and reposition on a cheap separate ticket (documented triple-surface DONE5: ATL//JFK, HTI//SYD, JRO//ZNZ). When coupons are tight, nest an out-and-back from an on-ticket hub instead (SYD-HTI return + DOH-ZNZ-DOH bought separately "saved 3 segments"). Third option: substitute a nearby city that has D (TLV→AMM) before burning a coupon. ([Booking & Pricing p96, Jul 2023](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/1776577-oneworld-booking-pricing-experiences-96.html); [AFF skylabsea DONE4, Sep 2023](https://www.australianfrequentflyer.com.au/community/threads/my-done4-oneworld-rtw-booking-experience.111025/))

**The first sector must be a flight; the fare origin is pinned to the first flight's country** `[multiple-reports]`
You cannot open with a surface to dodge an unwanted first flight — tools and agents have blocked it consistently since 2009. Position on a separate ticket instead. A trailing surface is fine only inside the Rule 4(c) zones. ([DONE4 ex-ICN thread, 2009](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/928670-done4-ex-icn-first-flight-surface-segment.html))

**SWP-origin itineraries can surface one ocean crossing — the theoretical answer to the transpacific D wall** `[corroborated-thin]`
Rule-valid and current, but verification found zero documented real-world uses. For SWP-origin clients facing transpacific D0: surface the Pacific (one mid-itinerary coupon), reposition on points/cheap J, concentrate D-hunting on the Atlantic. Non-SWP origins must fly both crossings. ([User Guide wiki](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2008084-oneworld-explorer-user-guide.html))

**Spare coupons are convertible: out-and-backs, a later free domestic round trip, status runs** `[community-consensus]`
FTers build to 16/16 deliberately — placeholder out-and-backs on free-changeable dates, the start-in-BOS-not-NYC pattern for a later "free" NYC round trip. Per-continent caps still bind. ([User Guide wiki](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2008084-oneworld-explorer-user-guide.html); [RTW-advice thread #65, Jul 2023](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2129083-advice-round-world-trip-include-australia-french-polynesia-5.html))

**Desk-level variance: some offices count a surface as TWO stopovers — pre-check under the hostile interpretation** `[multiple-reports]`
The consensus rule is one stopover at whichever end benefits the passenger, but offices have enforced stopovers at both ends (CX Colombo 2006; a QF phone agent 2011; a BA origin-continent count fight 2018). Surfaces in the origin continent are the danger zone (2-stopover cap). If a desk miscounts, don't argue — ticket what you can and reissue at a more knowledgeable office. ([Surface-sectors thread, 2006](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/595455-surface-sectors-continent-origin-2-stopover-rule.html))

### 1.5 Ticketing mechanics (plating, GDS, decay)

**Plating is the single highest-leverage decision — and it's a 12-month marriage** `[confirmed]`
The validating carrier exclusively controls all voluntary changes; cross-carrier takeover is dead (AA and QR both refuse QF-plated tickets; BA refused a UK resident's GBP-paid, BA-segment ticket because QF plated it). Choose at booking by expected change volume (AA = change-heavy), cost (QR via agent), status (BA only for BA Golds). The oneworld tool has defaulted nearly everything to QF stock since ~Feb 2024; since ~Aug 2024 a pre-payment screen lets you pick the first-segment carrier instead — never accept the QF default. Verify the e-ticket prefix immediately (001=AA, 081=QF, 125=BA, 157=QR). One documented escape: the AA desk took over a QF-plated ticket when the transaction PUT MONEY ON AA STOCK (DONE→AONE upgrade, overwater legs converted to AA codeshares) `[corroborated-thin]`. ([Bought-online-ticketed-QF thread, 2024-25](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2152193-oneworld-round-world-tickets-bought-online-ticketed-qantas.html); [Terrible fare thread, Mar 2024](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2155530-terrible-fare-changing-rtw-ticket-issued-qf.html); [LoyaltyLobby, May 2025](https://loyaltylobby.com/2025/05/12/reader-email-qantas-incapably-of-servicing-oneworld-rtw-tickets-it-issues/))

**QR plating is the YQ kill switch — fresh June 2026 proof** `[confirmed]`
The plating carrier determines which segments' YQ/YR are collected: QR validation collects YQ only on QR/BA/MH segments. June 2026 data point: a DONE6 ex-HND with 5 JL + 9 QR segments QR-plated carried $144 total YR vs $1,000-1,600 on comparable AA-ticketed ex-HND itineraries — after JL's new ~$315/segment fuel surcharge ("the QR plating effect"). QR has no RTW desk, won't issue or voluntarily change tickets — every change goes through your agent. Decision rule: stable itinerary = QR plating via Sabre/Amadeus agent; change-heavy = AA plating, eat the ~$1,000 premium. QR's YQ is front-loaded: after the first QR segment, extras add little — "either entirely avoid QR or go all in." ([Booking & Pricing p250, Jun 2026](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/1776577-oneworld-booking-pricing-experiences-250.html); [p74, Jan 2023](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/1776577-oneworld-booking-pricing-experiences-74.html); [Ex-OSL DONE4 Help p2, Jan 2025](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2176618-ex-osl-done-4-help-2.html))

**Shop the identical itinerary across plating carriers before paying** `[multiple-reports]`
Documented Sabre matrix on one BUD-origin DONE3: MH $6,247 / QR $6,275 / JL $6,293 / AY $6,500. The GDS doing the pricing matters as much as the stock — QR's own Amadeus-based system prices "part by part" and collects other carriers' YQ, so plate QR through a Sabre agent, not QR. ([Booking & Pricing p74, Jan 2023](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/1776577-oneworld-booking-pricing-experiences-74.html))

**QF/QR file whole-journey RTW YQ invisible to per-sector tools** `[confirmed]`
A single QF-coded DFW-SYD added ~USD 1,160 to an ex-OSL DONE4 (swap to AA code: $7,900→$6,740) while ITA Matrix showed NO surcharge on the sector standalone. The trap is origin-dependent — QF long-hauls carry near-zero YQ on ex-Japan tickets. Never validate segment costs in Matrix; autoprice the full RTW in a GDS and A/B-test marketing codes sector by sector. ([Carrier-imposed-fees thread, Jan 2023](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2107664-carrier-imposed-fees-done4-ex-osl.html); [User Guide p48+, 2025](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2008084-oneworld-explorer-user-guide-48.html))

**Sabre WPRW¥ autoprices most xONEx — agents don't know the entry; surfaces break it** `[community-consensus]`
Ordinary WP fails and agents wrongly declare the fare unpriceable. Literally tell a Sabre agent to use "WPRW¥". Sabre will not autoprice an itinerary containing a surface sector — expect manual fare construction and have a fallback desk lined up. ([Cheapest-place p4-5, Mar 2025](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2133996-cheapest-place-europe-one-world-explorer-fares-4.html); [Carrier-imposed-fees thread](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2107664-carrier-imposed-fees-done4-ex-osl.html))

**GDS-engine quirks decide which desk can even price you** `[confirmed]`
Sabre fails on MH codeshares operated by QR, on all-6-NA-segment builds, and miscounts continents (JFK-EZE-JFK jumping a count to "6"); Amadeus chokes on different constructions; an ex-India build refused to price unless CX was first carrier. If a desk says "fare not found": try the other GDS (AA=Sabre; BA/CX/QR=Amadeus), reorder so a pricing-friendly carrier (often CX) flies segment 1, or temporarily drop the offending stopover and re-add post-ticketing for $125. ([Booking & Pricing p200, Sep 2025](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/1776577-oneworld-booking-pricing-experiences-200.html))

**Desk arbitrage: the AA fares team tickets legal routings the tool rejects — and every desk misrefuses valid edge routings** `[confirmed]`
AA issued a DONE5 with a HNL-SEA surface backtrack the tool hard-rejected; conversely QF agents wrongly refused Morocco-as-Europe across three calls. When the tool rejects a routing you believe legal, don't redesign — take it to the AA desk. When an agent refuses, assume agent error before fare-rule error: quote the rule section, HUCA, or switch channels. ([Booking & Pricing p200, Aug 2025](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/1776577-oneworld-booking-pricing-experiences-200.html); [AFF RTW guide](https://www.australianfrequentflyer.com.au/oneworld-explorer-rtw-guide/))

**The oneworld online tool is unfit for surfaces and edge cases — treat its errors as bugs** `[confirmed]`
No way to enter a surface; hangs on valid itineraries; misapplies the Global Explorer 34,000-mile cap to uncapped xONEx; throws phantom "backtracking" errors; refuses QR as first carrier; carrier-level country-of-sale blocks exist invisibly (IB UIO-MAD). Re-validate any tool rejection against the fare rules (our validator) before redesigning; plan surfaces offline and ticket by phone/GDS agent. ([RTW-advice thread, Aug 2023](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2129083-advice-round-world-trip-include-australia-french-polynesia-5.html); [User Guide p56/p76, 2025](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2008084-oneworld-explorer-user-guide-76.html))

**Unticketed PNRs decay carrier-by-carrier: BA ~48h, QR <24h, AS fast** `[community-consensus]`
Partner robots drop segments on their own clocks while pricing drags; BA's back-office pricing round-trip alone has killed bookings ("by the time it came back the flights had been cancelled out"). Compress hold-to-ticket: payment ready before the PNR is built, ticket same-day, re-verify availability immediately before each pricing attempt. On BA, insist the PNR goes on the dedicated RTW fares queue processed by Manchester — the generic "fares no ticket issued" queue is the WRONG queue — and hand the agent the exact fare basis (DONE4) to shortcut the rate desk. ([BA-training thread p2, Jul 2023](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/british-airways-british-airways-club/2127821-oneworld-rtw-ba-training-2.html); [AFF skylabsea](https://www.australianfrequentflyer.com.au/community/threads/my-done4-oneworld-rtw-booking-experience.111025/))

**Cross-border purchase works but payment mechanics are a minefield** `[community-consensus]`
Anyone can buy ex-OSL by phone since ~2016, but: AA payment failed on a Norway→UK region switch; Australian cards routinely fail (pay via AA's local office); agents inconsistently charge NOK vs USD. Ask the rates desk to price in your card's currency; verify the converted amount against mid-market FX before paying (catches the agent accidentally charging the ex-LHR fare). ([Cheapest-place p2, Oct 2023](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2133996-cheapest-place-europe-one-world-explorer-fares-2.html); [Ex-OSL Help p2, Feb 2025](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2176618-ex-osl-done-4-help-2.html))

**Stale GDS-only fares are ADM bait** `[multiple-reports]`
A Sabre agency that ticketed ex-SEZ DONEs on a 2006-era BA-filed fare visible only in Sabre drew ~$5,000/ticket debit memos, cancellation threats, and weeks-long manual quotes for mid-journey reissues. Before paying for an aggressive-POS bargain via an agency: check the fare's filing date and that it prices in more than one GDS; prefer airline-direct for unusual origins (airlines don't ADM themselves). ([Should-I-offer-RTW-services thread, Apr 2024](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2156987-advice-should-i-offer-rtw-booking-services.html))

**Ticket numbers churn on every reissue — log them or lose retro credits** `[community-consensus]`
AA (and others) issue a new 13-digit number on many changes, including airline-initiated retimes, while sometimes merely revalidating; emails randomly cover only one passenger; partner retro-claims (notably AS) reject unless filed against the number active when THAT segment flew (one member needed his 3rd of 5 numbers). After every touch: record number + date (aa.com Manage Trips "i" icon, or boarding-pass barcode — the live ticket number is encoded in it). ([Keep-track-of-ticket-numbers thread, Jan 2025](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2182642-tip-keep-track-your-rtw-ticket-numbers-they-may-change.html))

**Cross-GDS reissue desync: after any exchange, partner systems hold stale e-ticket data for 24-48h+** `[confirmed]`
After an AA exchange, AY/RJ/QR sites showed old ticket numbers and BA phone claimed no visibility. After every change/schedule-change: wait 24-48h, verify e-ticket status segment-by-segment with each OPERATING carrier, and before check-in on any partner segment confirm the coupon shows the current number. A schedule-change email on a multi-carrier ticket is a fire alarm — call the plater and confirm reticketing completed. ([Booking & Pricing p74, Jan 2023](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/1776577-oneworld-booking-pricing-experiences-74.html))

**Agents mis-key 14-16 segment itineraries silently — verify the PNR after every call, never trust read-back** `[confirmed]`
Agents have read back the correct flight and booked a different one, and silently flipped stopovers to transits (a ticketed-point change that breaks fare validity). After any call: pull the PNR on operating carriers' sites and a GDS viewer (CheckMyTrip for Amadeus, virtuallythere for Sabre); verify flight numbers/dates, every stopover >24h and transit <24h, and that a new e-ticket receipt was actually emitted if a reissue was required. ([AFF skylabsea, Sep 2023](https://www.australianfrequentflyer.com.au/community/threads/my-done4-oneworld-rtw-booking-experience.111025/))

### 1.6 Failure modes and rescue

**The #1 catastrophic failure: plater misses the partner reticketing window → segments auto-cancel silently** `[confirmed]`
After any change or schedule change, if the plating carrier (most often QF) doesn't complete the reissue within ~24-48h, partner robots (CX has the shortest window) pull their segments from the PNR — including segments months away — discovered at check-in. Treat every change as incomplete until you hold a new e-ticket receipt; ask the agent to confirm the ticket was REISSUED (not just the PNR rebooked); re-verify all segments on each operating carrier's site at 24h and 72h. If segments vanish, demand reinstatement as an involuntary change (QF has done this). Working rescue paths: repeated calls until a supervisor conferences CX/oneworld; the plater's social-media team forcing a reissue. ([QF-ineptness thread, 2023](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2123999-seeking-advice-how-deal-qf-ineptness-oneworld-explorer-rtw-ticket.html); [AFF revenue-RTW-cancelled thread, Jul 2024](https://www.australianfrequentflyer.com.au/community/threads/revenue-rtw-booking-with-qantas-changed-now-segments-appear-cancelled.114619/))

**Cancellations generate no notification — self-monitoring is mandatory** `[confirmed]`
Schedule-change cancellations on multi-carrier tickets routinely reach no one (AA leg cancelled on an MH-plated LONE4 with zero notice; a QF reservation cancelled mid-trip discovered only on an unrelated call). Run a weekly PNR audit on EVERY operating carrier's website; set EF flight alerts on your own booked flights; after any schedule-change email verify coupon status before traveling. ([User Guide p46](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2008084-oneworld-explorer-user-guide-46.html); [Bought-online-ticketed-QF](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2152193-oneworld-round-world-tickets-bought-online-ticketed-qantas.html))

**Wrongly-demanded change fees die to verbatim rule text — in writing** `[confirmed]`
QF agents systematically charge $125 (sometimes per segment) for date-only changes that are free; one agent admitted "instructions are to charge anyway." Defeat sequence: quote 16(a)2.a ("changes permitted provided ticketed points remain the same") + the carrier's own schedule of fees (QF: no change fees in Business/First); HUCA across call centres; escalate to the X/Facebook DM team and paste the rule text — produced explicit written reversals. ACCC mention is escalation leverage. Know the boundary: connection-city changes and transit↔stopover flips ARE the $125. ([RTW-through-Qantas thread, May 2024](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2161590-rtw-ticket-through-qantas-but-they-charging-changing-fee.html); [Terrible fare thread, 2024](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2155530-terrible-fare-changing-rtw-ticket-issued-qf.html))

**Social-media DM teams out-perform call centres for reissues** `[community-consensus]`
QF's X/Facebook team has priced, changed, and force-reissued tickets the call centre failed on for a week ("QF Twitter team > Call center + support center"); X DMs work without paid verification; AA's Twitter team has the same reputation. Skip the call centre for QF-plated tickets: DM with PNR + exact flights + pasted rule paragraph; push past the initial "please call" deflection; allow 1-3 days. ([QF-ineptness thread, 2023](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2123999-seeking-advice-how-deal-qf-ineptness-oneworld-explorer-rtw-ticket.html))

**A recorded no-show lets the operating carrier strip ALL its segments — including ones months away** `[corroborated-thin]`
MH marked a passenger no-show (its own check-in desk relocation), then pulled every MH segment from the AA-plated ticket plus a random AA segment, stranding him overnight (AA RTW desk closed, regular res refused to act). If a no-show is ever recorded: immediately call the plater's RTW desk and have all downline segments of the affected carrier verified/reinstated before robots act; at the airport hunt for a supervisor. The Rule 3015 no-show fee is $125 — the real cost is the cascade. ([IRROPS thread, Dec 2024](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2178436-irrops-rtw-tickets.html))

**Mid-RTW IRROPS: the operating carrier owns the rebooking and often does nothing — carry a refundable backup for must-make legs** `[confirmed]`
AY provided literally zero assistance during a 2.5h delay threatening a last-flight-of-day connection; the passenger flew his pre-purchased refundable SK backup (~EUR 300) and claimed reimbursement. For tight or final-day connections near the END of a ticket: buy a cheap refundable non-oneworld backup and decide on the day. During disruption, propose specific replacement flights yourself (cite EU/UK261 Art 8 on European departures); know the walk-away fallback is abandoning the final coupon at near-zero cost. ([IRROPS thread, Nov 2024](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2178436-irrops-rtw-tickets.html))

**Mid-trip, ANY willing oneworld office can fix the ticket — shop downline, expect refusals as the norm** `[community-consensus]`
QR won't touch QF tickets (refused via every channel incl. the DOH transfer desk); an MH KUL office refused a CX-plated change and said "go talk to SQ" — the same change was done without fuss by JL at the next stop. JL offices have the best fix reputation; airport ticket desks of the plater often beat its call centre; frame requests with the exact rule paragraph. QF's Hobart centre (AU daytime via the AU domestic 13-number) is reputedly its most competent. ([QF-ineptness thread, 2023](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2123999-seeking-advice-how-deal-qf-ineptness-oneworld-explorer-rtw-ticket.html); [FAQ p95, 2016](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/338667-oneworld-explorer-ticket-faqs-95.html))

### CX (Cathay Pacific) — priority carrier

- **Trunk D/F hides behind the HKG through-connection** `[community-consensus]`. PROS O&D RM blocks discounted premium on LAX/SFO/JFK-HKG queried standalone, releases it married to a beyond point (JFK-HKG=D0, JFK-HKG-BKK=4 seats). Never have an agent quote a CX trunk as a standalone city pair — feed it as one O&D to a beyond point with a <24h connection, both legs in one transaction. ([r/awardtravel, Jun 2023](https://old.reddit.com/r/awardtravel/comments/13ym15k/booked_j_tickets_via_cathay_pacific/); [FT 2152207](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2152207-aa-rtw-desk-availability-compared-expertflyer-ow-online-booking-tool.html))
- **HKG stopover >24h divorces the marriage — cuts both ways** `[multiple-reports]`. Want a Hong Kong stopover? The trunk prices against blocked hub-local inventory — search alternates (LAX-TYO-HKG on JL). Don't want one? Keep the transit <24h and take the richer married inventory. If a desk says a connection is "unbookable together" while each leg shows space, lengthening past 24h makes the legs individually sellable. ([CX multi-carrier award thread, 2014](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/cathay-pacific-cathay/1581114-oneworld-multi-carrier-awards-sharing-ideas.html); [FAQ p174, 2024](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/338667-oneworld-explorer-ticket-faqs-174.html))
- **Sacrificial onward: book the married through-routing, drop the tail after the trunk coupon is FLOWN** `[multiple-reports]`. A pre-flight reissue re-runs availability and can lose the trunk D you captured; flown coupons are never re-evaluated. Tail drop = $125 routing change; if the segment you actually wanted opens later, the change is free. Make the sacrificial beyond point somewhere you genuinely want — no audit smell. Assembled from a UA/LH firsthand analogue + OWE change mechanics; no proven CX tail-drop on record. ([Sage-advice thread, 2011](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/united-mileage-plus-pre-merger/1189269-sage-advice-getting-award-space-married-segments.html); [RTW ex-OSL married thread, 2022](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/2141078-rtw-ex-osl-tight-positioning-married-segments-problems.html))
- **EF discrepancies cluster around HK public holidays — and CX's block is sometimes correct forecasting** `[community-consensus]`. CTS-HKG EF-D7 vs desk-1-seat just before 1 July; HKG-LHR at CNY never bookable and organically burned to D0/J0. Treat any CX segment within ~1 week of a HK holiday (CNY, Easter, 1 May, 1 Jul, early-Oct Golden Week, Christmas) as unverified until an agent has sold it; re-route or re-date rather than argue. ([Booking & Pricing p182, Feb 2025](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/1776577-oneworld-booking-pricing-experiences-182.html))
- **Per-partner inventory filtering, including last-premium-seat protection** `[multiple-reports]`. Since ~2024 AA/AS channels can't see CX space BA sometimes can; repeatable F2/A1 pattern where AA's desk cannot see the final A seat. The AA desk is escalating CX blocking "up the chain"; some agents will attempt a one-off inventory request to CX if asked. HUCA and try a BA/CX office before conceding. ([CX forum partner-awards thread, 2024](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/cathay-pacific-cathay/2150638-no-more-first-biz-awards-partners.html); [Booking & Pricing p182, 2025](https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/oneworld/1776577-oneworld-booking-pricing-experiences-182.html))
- **Release timing shifted post-2023: little at schedule opening, drip mid-schedule, real release ~2 weeks out** `[multiple-reports]`. Don't abandon a CX trunk on D0 at T-330 to T-60; book a placeholder with D now, set EF alerts, execute the free change ~10-14 days out. Does NOT apply across HK holidays. ## Part 3 — Booking playbook deltas

Concrete additions to the phone-booking scripts, `verify` workflow, and trip-management playbook. Imperative, in workflow order.

**Pre-call verification (rtw verify / scan-dates):**

1. When a trunk city-pair shows D0 standalone, ALWAYS re-run it in EF's connection search through the operating carrier's hub (and the reverse: re-check an EF-positive connection as separate legs) before recording a verdict. Record which form (married pair vs point-to-point) holds the D — that form is what the agent must sell, and the form you book is the form you're stuck with at change time.
2. Treat every EF D-count as a ceiling. Apply the discount: D7-D9 on BA/QR/CX/AY/JL wide-body trunk 90+ days out = bookable; D1-D2, hub-touching CX/QR/JL segment, QF/AS/UL metal, <30 days, or within a week of a HK public holiday = unverified until an agent sells it.
3. Do not cycle EF POS settings to explain a desk discrepancy — the filter is Point of Commencement and EF cannot see it. To verify under POC, build a dummy itinerary in the oneworld tool from the ACTUAL origin city, or use an Amadeus-equipped agent.
4. For JL segments: check I-class in EF as well as D — I predicts what a partner desk can actually sell. For a JL "no," ask the agent to test-sell in a fresh PNR to distinguish POC-block (unfixable) from agent error (HUCA).
5. For every blocked prime-code flight, enumerate its codeshare flight numbers in EF and check each marketing carrier's bucket — AA/IB/FJ codes on QR/QF metal are repeatedly the unlock. Note the tool can't book codeshares; only a phone agent can.
6. At the schedule edge (T-330 to T-361): a just-opened date showing D0 is not a verdict — re-scan 1-2 weeks later. AA/AS/WY (Sabre) can't even look past ~330 days; book placeholders inside the window instead of switching plater.
7. Fix our tooling: scan D first, then the rule-mandated fallback class (H on AA, B otherwise) — a D-only scan generates false "unbookable" verdicts on segments Rule 3015 lets you ticket in the fallback. **DONE 2026-06**: AA primary corrected to D + `rtw_fallback_class` added to carriers.yaml; `rtw verify` now re-scans the fallback on D0 and reports `→ fallback H2 (bookable)` (opt out with `--no-fallback`). Deeper economy keep-alive (L tier) remains a future enhancement.

**Booking call script:**

8. Book through the AA RTW desk (+1 800 247 3247, 7am Central for senior agents) or a specialist agent — never accept the oneworld tool's QF plating default. If using the tool, stop at the pre-payment ticketing-carrier screen and select the first-segment carrier.
9. Include one AA-CODED overwater segment when ticketing at the AA desk (a codeshare on partner metal qualifies, e.g. AA6918 LHR-YYZ op BA); swap it to the prime code post-issue for free. Without it, expect refusal.
10. Feed the agent segments one at a time in deliberate order — open/easy segments first, contested hub segments later — and feed married pairs as the exact two-flight combination in a single transaction. Never say "married segment" or "break the marriage"; say "can you work the combination" or "the system won't let you add a flight that shows available."
11. When availability looks wrong on a change call, ask the agent to strip any segments departing within 24h of the target from the working PNR (without ending the transaction), re-run availability, then re-add.
12. When the desk claims no space that every public tool shows, name the escalations explicitly: "can you request a one-off space release through your inventory procedure" and "can you raise this flight with your [JL/CX] liaison." Accept any offered waitlist (cost-free) but never let it substitute for a confirmed alternate.
13. Engineer segment 1 as a short, cheap, D-rich hop (AY OSL-HEL pattern) on a date you will definitely fly. Everything else can be a dummy date with D available. Never leave a segment in L "to fix later" if a fare rise is plausible — find any D date instead.
14. Before paying: have the same itinerary test-priced on every plausible plating stock (QR/JL/MH/AA minimum — Sabre quote spread was ~$250 on identical routing, QR-vs-BA gap EUR 769-1,273); ask the rates desk to price in your card's currency and sanity-check the FX against mid-market. If a Sabre agent says it won't price, tell them "WPRW¥".
15. Ticket same-day. Held PNRs decay: QR <24h, BA ~48h, AS fastest. On BA, insist the PNR goes to the dedicated RTW fares queue (Manchester), not the generic fares queue, and hand over the fare basis code.
16. Immediately after ticketing: verify the e-ticket stock prefix (001 AA / 081 QF / 125 BA / 157 QR), pull the PNR on a partner site (finnair.com shows ticket numbers reliably), and verify every flight number, date, and stopover-vs-transit status against what was read back. Read-back is not verification.

**Trip management / change protocol:**

17. Fly the first coupon as soon as practical to lock the base fare. Never touch segment 1's date pre-departure. Defer all optional changes until after coupon 1 is lifted.
18. Batch: accumulate every routing edit and execute in ONE call — $125 covers all simultaneous changes. If quoted per-segment, quote Rule 16 "per transaction."
19. Before any date change, recompute every affected connection against the 24h stopover line; bundle boundary-crossing moves with other paid changes, and never volunteer that a move converts a transit to a stopover.
20. Log every schedule-change notification as a free-change token. When you need a change the desk resists, attach it to a pending schedule change and have the whole event processed as involuntary — and propose your own reroute (different hub, different oneworld carrier, D verified) instead of accepting the auto-rebook.
21. After EVERY change: demand confirmation the ticket was REISSUED (new e-ticket receipt, not just PNR rebooked); at 24h and 72h verify each segment on each OPERATING carrier's site; record the new ticket number with date. A schedule-change email on a multi-carrier ticket is a fire alarm — call the plater and confirm partner reticketing completed before CX's short window expires.
22. Set EF alerts on D (and equipment type) for every segment you'd rather be on — equipment swaps and random close-in unblocks (≈14 and ≈8 days out) open space; BA sale windows flood D transiently (act within hours). When D opens on a leg you hold in L, call the plater for free cabin restoration (CX precedent: NO-ADC reissue).
23. On CX through-itineraries where the tail is sacrificial: drop the tail only AFTER the trunk coupon is flown ($125); a pre-flight reissue re-runs availability and can lose the trunk D.
24. If a no-show is ever recorded against you, call the plater's desk immediately and verify/reinstate all downline segments of that operating carrier before its robots strip them. Never voluntarily no-show any coupon except the final one; for the final coupon, fly carry-on-only on the penultimate leg and expect no refund.
25. If the plater stonewalls mid-trip: try the plater's social-media DM with pasted rule text (QF X/Facebook documented), then the next oneworld carrier's ticket office at your next stop (JL best reputation), then the plater's airport ticket desk. Expect refusals as the norm, not proof the change is impossible.
26. For must-make connections near the end of the ticket (low residual value), pre-buy a cheap refundable backup on a non-oneworld carrier and decide on the day; cite EU/UK261 Art 8 on European departures and propose specific flights.
27. If plans collapse mid-trip, never cancel: park remaining coupons ~11 months out with free date changes, then redirect them in one $125 transaction. Refund on abandonment is unused airport taxes at best.
