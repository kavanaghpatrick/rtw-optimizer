## The Current State of RTW Booking

The gap between ExpertFlyer availability and actual bookability is **wider than ever** in 2025-2026. Airlines are actively increasing POC-based, agency-level, and O&D restrictions on D/A inventory. The AA RTW desk's ability to see partner inventory is declining. Specialist travel agents are becoming more important, not less.

---

## 1. The Optimal Booking Strategy (Step by Step)

### Phase 1: Planning
1. **Choose origin city** — Oslo is cheapest in Europe (~$5,392 DONE4). Cairo fares are now **suspended** (EGP volatility). Tokyo and Johannesburg are alternatives.
2. **Optimize carrier mix** — Avoid QF long-haul (EUR 1,240 YQ on DFW-SYD). Use AA metal where possible. Never put BA on long-haul from LHR if AA operates the same route.
3. **Pre-verify D-class on ExpertFlyer** — but understand EF shows a ceiling, not reality. Flag hub transits (CX/HKG, QR/DOH) as uncertain.
4. **Identify married segment risks** — any <24hr connection on CX, QR, AY at their hub needs a connected routing strategy.

### Phase 2: Booking
5. **Use a specialist agent for QR plating** — saves EUR 700-1,300 vs AA/BA plating. Propeller Travel (UK, £80/ticket) or dutch_122/e-Businesstravel (Netherlands, EUR 75) are the top recommendations.
6. **Or call AA RTW desk** (+1 800-247-3247) for maximum flexibility — no service fees, liberal D-class, dedicated team. Best for complex itineraries where you'll make changes.
7. **Feed segments one at a time** — have exact flight numbers, dates, and routing ready. Don't let the agent search; you search and spoon-feed.
8. **Use dummy dates** for segments beyond 330 days — book with any date that has D-class, change for free later.
9. **Book hub transits as connected routings** — tell the agent "LAX to Bangkok via Hong Kong" not "LAX to Hong Kong, then Hong Kong to Bangkok."

### Phase 3: Post-Booking Optimization
10. **Fly the first segment early** to lock the base fare permanently.
11. **Set ExpertFlyer alerts** for segments booked in economy — upgrade for free when D opens.
12. **Monitor equipment changes** — these trigger D-class resets and can open availability.
13. **Bundle changes** into single calls — $125 is per transaction, not per segment.
14. **Exploit schedule changes** for free rerouting when airlines change times.

## 3. D-Class Workarounds When Unavailable

### Tier 1: Low Risk, High Success
- **Dummy dates** — book any date with D, free-change later. Still working in 2026.
- **Carrier substitution** — try codeshare numbers (AA code for same CX flight may show different D)
- **AA metal instead of partner** — on routes AA operates, their desk has better D access

### Tier 2: Medium Risk, Medium Success
- **Accept downgrade to economy** — book in Y, set EF alerts, re-book free when D opens
- **Different routing** — LAX-SEA-HKG instead of LAX-HKG if standalone is blocked
- **HUCA** — Hang Up, Call Again. Agent knowledge varies wildly at every desk.

### Tier 3: Higher Risk
- **Close-in releases** — unreliable. JL may release at ~90 days, QR only 2 A-class seats, BA flash availability during sales.
- **Travel waivers during disruptions** — exploitable for free rerouting (documented Mar 2026)
- **Connected routing to unlock hub O&D** — book onward from hub to unlock D-class, change later ($125)

## 5. What's Changed in 2025-2026

### Getting Harder
- BA deliberately restricting D-class on own routes for RTW
- JAL actively blocking RTW bookings through AA
- POC-based restrictions increasing (ex-Japan, ex-Egypt worst affected)
- Married segment enforcement tightening across CX, QR
- AA desk losing visibility into partner inventory
- Post-COVID knowledge drain at airline call centers

### Getting Easier
- Asia two-visit rule liberalized (2023+)
- Fiji Airways and Oman Air joined oneworld (new routing options)
- Free date changes still working
- AA codeshare flexibility improved
- Equipment changes trigger D-class resets (worth monitoring)
- CX plating emerging as cheaper alternative

### New Discoveries (Last 6 Months)
- **Economy search first** workaround for online tool's 34K-mile bug (Jan 2026)
- **Travel waivers** exploitable during disruptions (Mar 2026)
- **Ticket validity**: 12 months from departure date, not purchase — effective 2-year window with dummy dates
- **CargoHoldFlyer** on FlyerTalk offering Sabre GDS access for $150/booking
