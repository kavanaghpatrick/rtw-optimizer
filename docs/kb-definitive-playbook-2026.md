## How to Use This Document

# Phase 1: Planning

*Goal: Design a routing that is Rule 3015 compliant, cost-optimized, and has the highest probability of being bookable in D-class.*

## 1.1 Choose Your Origin City

The origin city is the single largest cost lever. The base fare difference between the cheapest and most expensive origins exceeds $5,000.

### Origin City Ranking (DONE4, Business, 4 Continents)

| Rank | Origin | Base Fare (USD) | Currency | Practical Notes |
|------|--------|-----------------|----------|-----------------|
| 1 | Cairo (CAI) | ~$4,000 | EGP | Cheapest globally. Fares currently suspended due to EGP volatility. |
| 2 | Johannesburg (JNB) | ~$5,000 | ZAR | Good if Africa is on your route. Expensive positioning from Europe. |
| 3 | Colombo (CMB) | ~$5,200 | LKR | SriLankan is oneworld. Niche. |
| 4 | **Oslo (OSL)** | **~$5,400** | **NOK** | **Best balance of price + accessibility for UK/European travelers.** |
| 5 | Delhi (DEL) | ~$5,543 | INR | Good QR connectivity. |
| 6 | Stockholm (ARN) | ~$6,177 | SEK | ~$777 more than Oslo. No advantage. |
| 7 | Tokyo (NRT) | ~$6,360 | JPY | Weak yen helps. Only worthwhile if already in Asia. |
| 8 | London (LHR) | ~$8,000 | GBP | High base + GBP 253 APD. Convenient but expensive. |
| 9 | New York (JFK) | ~$10,500 | USD | Most expensive conventional origin. |
| 10 | Zurich (ZRH) | ~$11,241 | CHF | Worst in Europe. |

### Why Oslo Is the Default Choice (UK/European Travelers)

Oslo saves approximately **$2,920 per person** vs London on a DONE4 ticket:

| Component | London (LHR) | Oslo (OSL) | Saving |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|--------|
| Base fare | $8,000 | $5,400 | **$2,600** |
| UK APD (J-class long-haul) | ~$330 | $0 | **$330** |
| Norwegian departure tax | $0 | ~$10 | -$10 |
| **Subtotal** | **$8,330** | **$5,410** | **$2,920** |

Even after a GBP 100 round-trip positioning flight LHR-OSL, net saving is **$2,660 per person**. For two travelers: over $5,000.

**Positioning options**: BA Avios (4,000-7,500 + ~GBP 30 taxes), Norwegian Air (GBP 30-80), Ryanair (GBP 20-60), SAS (GBP 60-150). Book separately -- this is NOT part of the RTW ticket. Arrive the day before your first RTW segment and build in a buffer.

### Currency Timing

Fares are filed in local currency and converted at the prevailing exchange rate. NOK has been structurally weak against USD/GBP since 2020. The optimal booking window is when NOK is at a local low and the IATA fare filing has not been updated recently. After booking, fly the first segment immediately to lock the fare -- subsequent currency movements will not affect your ticket.

## 1.2 Design Your Routing

### Rule 3015 Core Constraints

| Rule | Limit |
|------|-------|
| Maximum segments | 16 (including surface sectors) |
| Per-continent segments | 4 (6 for North America) |
| Intercontinental departures | 1 per continent (exceptions exist) |
| Stopovers per continent | 2 (after departing origin continent) |
| Direction of travel | Continuous forward between Tariff Conferences (TC1/TC2/TC3) |
| Backtracking | Cannot return to a previously visited TC (with defined exceptions) |
| Return to origin | Must return to origin city |

### First Segment Strategy

The first segment is the most important segment on the ticket. It must be:

1. **On a real date you can fly within days of booking** -- NEVER a dummy date
2. **Short and cheap** to minimize YQ and taxes
3. **On a low-YQ carrier** -- Finnair (AY, ~$10 YQ) is ideal

**The Oslo standard**: OSL-HEL on Finnair. Operates multiple times daily, good D-class availability, ultra-low YQ, connects to the entire AY long-haul network from Helsinki.

**Why this matters**: Flying the first segment permanently locks your base fare. If you delay and the fare increases before you fly it, any change to the first segment triggers repricing at the new (higher) rate. One FlyerTalk user reported a $6,000+ repricing hit from changing their first segment date before flying it.

### Carrier Selection: The YQ Hierarchy

Carrier-imposed surcharges (YQ/YR) are the single largest variable cost after the base fare. Choose carriers deliberately.

| Tier | Carriers | YQ/Segment | Use For |
|------|----------|-----------|---------|
| **Very Low** | AY (~$10), JL (~$12), FJ (~$20) | $10-20 | Maximize these. AY for Europe-Asia, JL for transpacific, FJ for SWP. |
| **Low** | AS (~$40), AA (~$50, $0 domestic), MH (~$60), WY (~$90) | $40-90 | Good alternatives. AA for US domestic (zero YQ). |
| **Medium** | QR (~$150), CX (~$200) | $150-200 | Acceptable. QR has non-linear pricing (first segment carries bulk of charge). |
| **High** | IB (~$220), BA (~$321), QF (~$334) | $220-334 | **Avoid where possible.** BA+APD is punishing. QF is the worst. |

### Carrier Substitution by Route

| Route Type | Best Carrier | Avoid | Saving |
|-----------|-------------|-------|--------|
| Transatlantic | AA ($50) or AY ($10) | BA ($321 + APD) | $270-580/segment |
| Transpacific | JL ($12) or AA ($50) | QF ($334) | $280-320/segment |
| Europe-Asia | AY ($10) via HEL | BA ($321) or AY HEL-NRT JV ($270-480 surcharge!) | $260-470/segment |
| Intra-Asia | JL ($12) or MH ($60) | CX ($200) | $140-190/segment |
| US Domestic | AA ($0) | -- | Zero YQ on AA domestic |

**Critical trap**: AY Helsinki-Tokyo (HEL-NRT) carries a Joint Venture surcharge of $270-480, making it far more expensive than it appears. Use JL for Japan instead.

**QF warning**: Never book QF on long-haul. QF DFW-SYD adds approximately EUR 1,240 in surcharges for a single segment. The AA codeshare on the same physical flight saves ~$1,160. Community consensus: "Never get a QF-plated RTW."

### Codeshare Optimization

The **marketing carrier** (2-letter code on your ticket) determines YQ, not the operating carrier. Same seat, same plane, different price.

| Physical Flight | Marketing Code | YQ Impact |
|----------------|---------------|-----------|
| BA-operated LHR-SEA | BA code | ~$321 + APD |
| BA-operated LHR-SEA | AA code | ~$50 |
| QF-operated DFW-SYD | QF code | ~$334 |
| QF-operated DFW-SYD | AA code | ~$50 |

**Ask the agent**: "Can this segment be booked under [carrier] code?" AA desk agents do not systematically try codeshares -- you must request specific codes.

**Gotcha**: Schedule changes silently revert codeshare codes to the prime operating carrier code, undoing your YQ optimization. Monitor after any schedule change and re-request the codeshare.

### Plating Carrier Decision

The plating (validating) carrier determines YQ across the ENTIRE ticket. This is the airline whose ticket stock is used.

| Plating Carrier | Total YQ (16-seg DONE4) | Flexibility | How to Book |
|----------------|------------------------|-------------|-------------|
| **QR** | ~$800 | Low (no RTW desk) | Must use travel agent |
| **MH** | ~$900 | Low | Limited RTW experience |
| **CX** | ~$1,500 | Medium | CX offices worldwide |
| **AA** | ~$1,800 | **High** (gold standard desk) | Call +1-800-247-3247 |
| **BA** | ~$2,500 | Very low (email-only, weeks of delay) | Avoid |

**The trade-off**: QR plating saves EUR 800-1,273 vs AA plating on identical routings (documented by dutch_122). But QR has no RTW desk -- all changes go through your travel agent. If your agent is unreachable during a disruption at 2 AM in Fiji, you are stranded. AA plating costs more but gives you a direct line to the most competent RTW desk in the alliance.

**Decision rule**: Simple, stable itineraries = QR plating via agent. Complex itineraries with likely changes = AA plating.

### APD Avoidance (UK Travelers)

UK Air Passenger Duty (APD) adds GBP 244-253 per premium-class departure from a UK airport (after 24+ hours on the ground). Key rules:

- **Transit under 24 hours on the same ticket = NO APD** -- this is the critical exemption
- Originating from a non-UK city (Oslo) = no APD on the first departure
- Arriving in the UK is free (no landing APD)

**Strategies**:
1. Originate from Oslo (saves base fare + APD)
2. If routing through LHR mid-trip, keep ground time under 24 hours (transit, not stopover)
3. If a UK stopover is unavoidable, depart on a short-haul European segment (Band A = GBP 32) rather than long-haul (Band B/C = GBP 244-253)
4. Fly INTO the UK as your arrival point, never depart from it on a premium long-haul

## 1.3 Identify Risk Segments

Before you start verifying availability, flag segments that have a high probability of booking failure.

### High-Risk Segment Types

| Risk Type | Example | Why | Mitigation |
|-----------|---------|-----|-----------|
| Hub transit on CX | LAX-HKG (terminating at hub) | CX O&D control blocks standalone D-class to/from HKG | Book as connected routing (LAX-BKK via HKG) |
| Hub transit on QR | Any DOH connection | QR married segment control; even >24hr stopovers may not break it | Book onward from DOH, or try standalone with segment-by-segment method |
| JL from AA desk | Any JL segment | JL systematically blocks A/D for AA-ticketed RTW | Try AA codeshare number; consider JL close-in release (~90 days) |
| QF long-haul | SYD-LAX, SYD-DFW | QF stingy with D-class AND highest YQ | Use AA codeshare or re-route via JL transpacific |
| AA transcontinental | JFK-LAX, JFK-SFO | AA blocks A/D on transcon for RTW fares | Route via MIA-BOS-LAX or use alternative city pairs |
| BA competitive routes | Routes BA also flies | BA restricts partner D-class when BA operates the same route | Avoid BA plating; use AA code on BA metal |

### The O&D Revenue Management Problem

Modern airlines use Origin-Destination revenue management. The **same physical seat** on the same flight has different availability depending on the passenger's full journey:

- **LAX-HKG standalone** (local passenger): D-class BLOCKED -- CX protects premium HKG-bound seats
- **LAX-CTU via HKG** (connecting passenger): D-class RELEASED -- CX wants this through-traffic

This is why ExpertFlyer shows D9 but the agent cannot book. ExpertFlyer queries standalone availability. The agent's system queries with full O&D context, triggering different (more restrictive) results.

**Practical implication**: For any segment through CX/HKG or QR/DOH, present it to the agent as a connected routing ("I'd like to fly from LAX to Bangkok via Hong Kong"), not as a standalone segment to the hub.

# Phase 2: Pre-Booking Verification

*Goal: Confirm D-class availability on every segment before you call the booking desk, while understanding the limitations of what you can verify.*

## 2.1 ExpertFlyer: What to Trust, What to Flag

ExpertFlyer is the best publicly available tool for D-class checking, but it shows a ceiling, not reality. Community reports indicate EF availability matches agent-bookable availability roughly **70-85% of the time**. The remaining 15-30% of discrepancies are not random -- they follow predictable patterns.

### The Availability Stack

What ExpertFlyer shows is Layer 3 of a 9-layer filtering pipeline:

```
Layer 1: Raw cabin inventory (physical seats)
Layer 2: Booking class allocation (RM decision)
Layer 3: GDS availability feed  <-- ExpertFlyer reads here
Layer 4: Point of Sale (POS) filter
Layer 5: Point of Commencement (POC) filter  <-- EF CANNOT see this
Layer 6: O&D filter (origin-destination pair)  <-- EF CANNOT see this
Layer 7: Married segment control  <-- EF CANNOT see this
Layer 8: Agency-level restrictions
Layer 9: Fare-type restrictions (RTW vs revenue vs award)
```

**EF shows Layer 3. The agent books at Layer 9.** Five additional filters can block what EF shows as available.

### EF Confidence Levels

| Confidence | Conditions | Meaning |
|-----------|-----------|---------|
| **HIGH** | D7-D9, carrier in Good tier (AA domestic, FJ, RJ), >90 days out, no MSC risk | Very likely bookable |
| **MEDIUM** | D3-D6, or carrier in Moderate tier (QF, BA, MH), or 30-90 days out | Probably bookable but verify quickly |
| **LOW** | D1-D2, or carrier in Problematic tier (CX, QR, JL), or <30 days, or MSC flagged | May vanish before agent can book |
| **CAVEAT** | Any result where POC mismatch is likely (non-US origin with USA POS) | EF result is unreliable |

### Carrier Reliability Tiers on ExpertFlyer

| Tier | Carriers | EF Accuracy | Notes |
|------|----------|-------------|-------|
| **Good** (~90%) | AA (domestic), FJ, RJ | Reliable | AA domestic segments rarely have POC issues |
| **Moderate** (~75%) | QF, BA, MH | POC-dependent | BA restricts partner D on own routes |
| **Problematic** (~60-70%) | CX, QR, JL | Hub-married + POC + capacity restrictions | Expect failures at booking |

### What EF Cannot Detect

| Factor | Can EF Detect? | Workaround |
|--------|---------------|------------|
| Raw D-class count | Yes | -- |
| POS restriction | Partial (can change POS) | Try multiple POS settings |
| POC restriction | **No** | Must verify with agent |
| Married segment control | Partial (advanced connection search) | Search as connection, not point-to-point |
| Agency restriction | **No** | Must verify with agent |
| Fare-type block (RTW-specific) | **No** | Must verify with agent |
| Capacity limitation clause | **No** | Must verify with agent |

### EF POS Setting

Our scraper defaults to "USA (Default)" POS. Community testing shows changing POS has **less impact than expected** because the real differentiator is POC, not POS. One user reported identical D7 results across UK, Norway, and India POS settings.

**EF does not support POC filtering at all.** This is the single biggest limitation. Only Amadeus terminal access (not even Sabre) can query by POC.

## 2.2 The Verification Strategy

### Step 1: Screen All Segments on ExpertFlyer

Run D-class checks on every segment. Record the count and note:
- Is it a hub transit (CX/HKG, QR/DOH)?
- Is the carrier in the Problematic tier?
- Is the connection under 24 hours (married segment risk)?
- Is it close-in (<30 days)?

### Step 2: Flag Segments by Risk

Categorize each segment:

| Category | Action |
|---------|--------|
| **Green** (D5+, Good-tier carrier, no MSC risk) | Proceed with confidence |
| **Yellow** (D1-4, or Moderate-tier, or slight MSC risk) | Proceed but have backup dates/carriers ready |
| **Red** (Problematic carrier, hub transit, or <D3 close-in) | Prepare alternatives before calling |

### Step 3: Run Connection Searches for Hub Transits

For CX/HKG and QR/DOH segments, use ExpertFlyer's advanced mode to search the full connection (e.g., LAX-BKK via HKG, not just LAX-HKG). This can reveal married segment availability that standalone searches miss.

### Step 4: Cross-Check Critical Segments via xONEx

For Red segments, build a dummy RTW on the oneworld online booking tool starting from your actual origin city. This tests Layer 9 availability directly. One user confirmed: "Just tried a dummy ATW booking on the OW website starting in UK and there was no issue selecting the flight."

### Step 5: Have Alternatives Ready

For every Red segment, prepare:
- 2-3 alternative dates within the same week
- At least 1 alternative carrier on the same route
- At least 1 alternative routing (different city pair)

## 2.3 Set Up ExpertFlyer Alerts

For segments you cannot yet verify (beyond the booking window) or where availability is marginal:
- Create D-class alerts on ExpertFlyer for your target flights
- EF will email you when D-class opens or changes
- Particularly useful for monitoring BA sales (which trigger wholesale D-class releases) and equipment changes (which reset D-class allocations)

# Phase 3: Booking

*Goal: Successfully get every segment confirmed in D-class, with the right carrier codes, at the optimal price.*

## 3.1 Choose Your Booking Channel

### Option A: AA RTW Desk (Best for Flexibility)

**Phone**: +1-800-247-3247 (US toll-free) / +1-817-267-1151 (international)
**Hours**: Mon-Fri 0700-2230 CT, Sat-Sun 0700-2000 CT
**Cost**: Free (no service fee)
**Best call time**: 7 AM DFW time (CT) for the most experienced agents

**Strengths**: Dedicated RTW team, real-time validation during the call, agents know Rule 3015, best for mid-trip changes, AA.com visibility of itinerary.
**Weaknesses**: Higher YQ than QR plating (~$1,000 more), desk hours limited, calls take 30-60 minutes.

### Option B: Specialist Travel Agent (Best for Cost)

| Agent | Location | Fee | Plating | Specialty |
|-------|----------|-----|---------|-----------|
| **Propeller Travel (danielbk)** | UK | GBP 80/ticket + GBP 35/reissue | QR | YQ optimization, UK travelers |
| **dutch_122 / e-Businesstravel** | Netherlands | EUR 75/ticket | QR | Maximum YQ savings, power-user clientele |

**Strengths**: QR plating saves EUR 800-1,273 vs AA, experienced with complex RTW.
**Weaknesses**: All changes must go through agent. No direct desk for emergencies. Agent availability varies.

### Option C: CX Offices (Emerging Alternative)

CX offices handle RTW by email. Good paper trail, sometimes waive fees. One user made 3 changes with CX, none charged -- including one that should have cost $125.

### What to Avoid

- **BA**: Offline fares team with 2-day turnaround. Email-only changes. Highest YQ. Not recommended.
- **QF**: Agents described as "hopeless" post-pandemic. Have cancelled reservations mid-call. Charge for free changes. Disputed Rule 16 text with customers. "Never get a QF-plated RTW."
- **The oneworld online tool**: "Very buggy." Defaults to QF ticketing for ex-Europe bookings. Use desks or agents instead.

## 3.2 The Booking Call: Segment-by-Segment Method

This is the gold-standard technique from FlyerTalk, perfected over a decade of community experience.

### Before the Call

Prepare a complete written list with:
- Every segment: origin, destination, carrier, flight number, date, cabin class
- Flag hub transits with the connected routing (e.g., "LAX to Bangkok via Hong Kong")
- Flag codeshare preferences (e.g., "AA code on BA-operated LHR-SEA")
- Know which segments are on real dates and which are dummy dates
- Have 2-3 backup dates for each segment

### During the Call

1. **Identify yourself**: "I'd like to book a oneworld Explorer round-the-world ticket, business class, four continents, originating from Oslo."

2. **Feed segments one at a time**: Give the agent ONE flight at a time. Do not present the full itinerary upfront. Wait for D-class confirmation before moving to the next segment.

3. **Never present two segments as a connection**: Say "I'd like to fly Cathay Pacific from Hong Kong to Tokyo Narita on March 20" -- NOT "I'd like to connect through Hong Kong." Presenting segments as connections triggers married segment restrictions in the GDS.

4. **Exception for hub transits**: When you NEED the connected routing to unlock O&D-restricted availability (CX/HKG, QR/DOH), tell the agent: "I'd like to fly from Los Angeles to Bangkok via Hong Kong on Cathay Pacific." This presents it as a connected journey, which is what unlocks the D-class that would be blocked standalone.

5. **Request specific codeshare codes**: "Can this be booked under the AA codeshare number?" The agent will not try this on their own.

6. **Use dummy dates for far-future segments**: "The date for this segment is a placeholder -- I'll call back to change it to the actual date once the schedule opens."

7. **If D-class is unavailable on a segment**: Have the agent try the codeshare number, try adjacent dates (+/-1-3 days), try an alternative carrier on the same route, or accept the segment in economy and set up EF alerts to catch D-class when it opens.

8. **Confirm carrier codes before ending the call**: Read back every segment with its marketing carrier code. AA may auto-change codes after you hang up.

### After the Call

- **Verify the full itinerary within 24 hours** on AA.com or by calling back
- **Check marketing carrier codes** -- schedule changes can silently revert codeshares to the prime carrier
- **Note your PNR number and e-ticket number** -- store offline
- **Pay immediately** to lock the fare

## 3.3 Hub Transit Strategy

For segments through CX/HKG or QR/DOH, the connected routing approach is essential but carries risks.

### The Connected Routing Technique

If standalone LAX-HKG shows D0 at booking but LAX-BKK via HKG shows D-class:

1. Book LAX-BKK via HKG (two segments: LAX-HKG + HKG-BKK)
2. Both segments get D-class because the airline sees a connecting passenger
3. If you actually want to stop in HKG, ensure the connection is >24 hours (stopover) if possible
4. If you want to drop the onward HKG-BKK later, this is a $125 routing change

### The "Sacrificial Onward" Risk

Adding a dummy onward segment to unlock hub availability, then dropping it later:

| Action | Cost | Risk |
|--------|------|------|
| Add onward to unlock D-class | Included in initial booking | None |
| Drop onward later ($125 routing change) | $125 | Medium -- reissue may trigger fresh O&D check, potentially losing the D-class |
| Fly first leg, then drop onward | $125 | **Safe** -- coupon is consumed, no re-evaluation |
| QR specifically monitors for dummy segments | -- | **High** -- QR flags this as POC violation |

**Safer alternative**: Make the onward segment somewhere you actually want to visit. Route productively, not sacrificially.

## 3.4 Dummy Dates

For segments beyond the ~355-day booking window, or where D-class is unavailable on your preferred date, use placeholder dates.

### The Rules

| Rule | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Date changes are **free** | No fee, no repricing (except first segment before departure) |
| First segment: **NEVER a dummy** | Book on a real date, fly immediately to lock fare |
| D-class must exist on the dummy date | Cannot book without confirmed inventory |
| Chronological order required | Dummy dates must maintain segment sequence in the GDS |
| 12-month validity from first flight | All segments must complete within this window |

### Practical Execution

1. Design your full routing (all cities, carriers, directions)
2. Book segment 1 on a real date you can fly THIS WEEK
3. Book near-term segments (within booking window) on real dates
4. Book far-future segments on dummy dates -- any date with D-class, clustered near the edge of the booking window
5. Issue the ticket and pay

### The Cascade Problem

When changing a dummy date to a real date, subsequent dummy dates may need to move too (GDS requires chronological ordering). Work forward through the itinerary incrementally. When you change segment 5 from dummy to real, also move segment 6's dummy date to be chronologically after the new real date.

# Phase 4: Post-Booking

*Goal: Lock the fare permanently, optimize the ticket, and prepare for the rolling date-change process.*

## 4.1 Fly the First Segment Immediately

**This is the single most important action after booking.**

When you fly the first segment:
- The base fare is **permanently locked** at the rate you booked
- All subsequent changes (dates, routing) only recalculate taxes and surcharges, NOT the base fare
- You start the 12-month validity clock (all segments must be completed within 12 months)

When you have NOT flown the first segment:
- ANY change to the first segment (even a date change) can trigger full repricing
- ANY routing change can trigger repricing if the published fare has increased
- You are exposed to fare increases between booking and flying

**Timeline**: Book Monday, fly Tuesday. The closer to booking, the less fare-increase risk.

## 4.2 Set Up Monitoring

### ExpertFlyer Alerts

For every segment booked on a dummy date or in economy (waiting for D-class upgrade):
- Set a D-class availability alert on ExpertFlyer
- EF will email when D-class opens on your target dates
- Particularly valuable for JL routes (D released ~90 days out) and BA (D released during sales)

### Schedule Change Monitoring

Check your itinerary on AA.com weekly. Airlines make silent schedule changes that can:
- Cancel or retime flights (creating cascading issues)
- Change equipment (which resets D-class allocations -- sometimes D opens on new equipment)
- Revert codeshare codes to the prime carrier (undoing YQ optimization)

### Equipment Change Opportunities

When airlines swap aircraft on a route, the D-class allocation resets. This can suddenly open availability on previously blocked routes. Monitor for equipment changes on your most difficult segments.

## 4.3 Rolling Date Changes

As real travel dates enter the booking window (~330-355 days out), call the AA RTW desk (or your agent) to change dummy dates to actual dates.

### Process

1. Check D-class availability on your target date BEFORE calling
2. Have 2-3 backup dates ready
3. Call the AA RTW desk: "I'd like to change the date on my Oslo-to-Helsinki segment"
4. Each date change is **free** (not a routing change)
5. Confirm the change was processed and verify the full itinerary
6. Allow 24-48 hours for cross-system synchronization, then verify with operating carriers

### Bundling

You can change multiple dates in a single call. This saves time (each call takes 15-30 minutes). The agent cancels each old segment and sells the new one, then reissues the ticket.

### Date Change Calendar

Create a personal timeline of when each dummy-date segment's real travel date enters the booking window:

```
2026-04-10:  BKK-HKG segment (target: Aug 2026 travel) enters window -- call to change
2026-05-15:  HKG-NRT segment (target: Sep 2026 travel) enters window -- call to change
2026-06-20:  NRT-LAX segment (target: Oct 2026 travel) enters window -- call to change
```

## 4.4 Economy-to-Business Upgrades

If any segment was booked in economy (D-class unavailable at booking time):
- Set ExpertFlyer alerts for D-class on that route
- When D opens, call the desk and request a free carrier/date change to get the D-class seat
- Carrier substitution on the same route is free (e.g., switch SIN-HKG from CX to MH if MH has D-class)

## 4.5 Post-Booking Cost Optimization

After the fare is locked (first segment flown), you can make routing changes ($125 per transaction) that improve YQ:

- Replace a QF long-haul segment with an AA codeshare on the same route: save ~$1,160
- Replace a BA segment with an AY segment on a different route: save ~$310/segment
- Bundle all desired routing changes into ONE call to pay only ONE $125 fee
- Schedule changes by airlines give you free rerouting leverage (fee waived for involuntary changes)

# Phase 5: Mid-Trip

*Goal: Handle changes, disruptions, and optimizations while traveling.*

## 5.1 The Golden Rules for Mid-Trip

1. **NEVER no-show without calling first.** If you miss a segment without calling, all downstream segments are automatically cancelled within minutes. This is the coupon-order rule and it is merciless.

2. **Call BEFORE the scheduled departure time.** Once departure passes, the no-show cascade may already be triggered.

3. **Always call the AA RTW desk for changes** (if AA-plated). They are the most competent. Budget 30-60 minutes per call.

4. **Track your ticket number after every change.** AA issues new ticket numbers after most changes (even minor schedule adjustments). The correct ticket number is essential for mileage credit claims. One user had 5 different ticket numbers across their trip and had to identify ticket number 3 out of 5 to get Alaska Airlines mileage credit.

5. **Carry a printed itinerary offline** with PNR, e-ticket numbers, and contact numbers for the AA RTW desk.

## 5.2 Change Fee Summary

| Change | Fee | Conditions |
|--------|-----|-----------|
| Date/time change (any segment) | **Free** | Unlimited times, subject to D-class availability |
| Carrier change (same route) | **Free** | E.g., SIN-HKG from CX to MH |
| Flight number change | **Free** | Earlier/later flight, same day, same carrier |
| Add/remove a city | **$125** per transaction | Bundle all routing changes into one call |
| Reorder cities | **$125** per transaction | -- |
| Drop a segment | **$125** per transaction | -- |
| Add a segment | **$125** per transaction | Within 16-segment limit |
| Stopover/transfer conversion | **$125** per transaction | -- |
| Add a continent | **Repriced** | Full fare recalculation to higher tier |
| Upgrade cabin | **Repriced** | Fare difference charged |
| Downgrade cabin | **$125** | No refund of fare difference |

**Critical**: The $125 fee is per TRANSACTION (one phone call), not per segment. Making 4 routing changes in one call = $125 total. Making the same changes across 2 calls = $250.

## 5.3 Schedule Change Leverage

When an airline involuntarily changes your schedule (flight retimed, cancelled, equipment swap), you gain leverage:

1. **Free routing changes**: The $125 fee is waived for involuntary changes
2. **Consolidate desired changes**: Bundle changes you wanted to make anyway with the involuntary rerouting -- all changes become free
3. **Document the change**: Screenshot the schedule change notification as evidence in case the agent disputes the fee waiver
4. **Travel waivers during disruptions**: Exploitable for free rerouting (documented working as recently as March 2026)

## 5.4 Segment Dropping

### Dropping a Middle Segment

You CAN remove a middle segment, but:
- Call BEFORE the departure time (otherwise all downstream segments are cancelled)
- This is a routing change ($125 fee)
- The remaining itinerary must still be Rule 3015 compliant

### Dropping the Last Segment

**Practically tolerated, never enforced.** No FlyerTalk member has reported fare recalculation for skipping the final segment. The theoretical risk (airline recalculates point-to-point fares for all segments flown) exists but has never materialized in community data.

If you plan to skip the last segment:
- Travel carry-on only on the penultimate flight (checked bags would be tagged through)
- Ensure you have appropriate visa/entry clearance for your actual final destination
- The base fare is already locked after flying the first segment

### Never Cancel Mid-Trip

Cancelling an RTW ticket after partial use triggers the refund trap: the airline recalculates the value of segments flown at one-way point-to-point published fares. Because business class one-ways can cost $3,000-8,000+ per segment, the "used value" easily exceeds the total RTW fare. Result: zero refund, plus cancellation penalty.

**Instead**: Keep the ticket active. Change remaining segments to destinations you want ($125), rebook to far-future dates (free), or simply let it expire.

## 5.5 Handling Disputes with Airlines

### When an Agent Refuses a Free Change

1. **Quote Rule 16**: "Rule 16(a)2.a states changes are permitted provided ticketed points remain the same. This is a date change -- ticketed points are unchanged. No fee should apply."
2. **HUCA**: Hang Up, Call Again. Agent knowledge varies wildly.
3. **Use written channels**: Twitter/X DM teams are often more knowledgeable than phone agents. They must actually check rules before responding in writing.
4. **For QF specifically**: Have four browser windows open: fare rules (from purchase date), QF Conditions of Carriage, QF Schedule of Fees (which states "No Change Booking Fees will apply for changes to a booking in Business or First"), and your change log.

### Involuntary Downgrades

If you are involuntarily downgraded on a segment:
1. Get written confirmation of the involuntary nature
2. Call the AA RTW desk immediately to request rebooking (free date/carrier change to restore business class)
3. If no D-class is available, fly in economy and claim compensation under EU261 (EU departures, EUR 250-600) or US DOT rules

## 5.6 IRROPS (Irregular Operations)

When disruptions hit:
- **Missed connection (airline's fault)**: Operating carrier must rebook you. Contact them at the airport.
- **Airport closed / natural disaster**: IRROPS protection applies. Airline must rebook at no charge.
- **Medical emergency**: Call AA desk, request date change (free), get medical documentation.
- **Want to extend a stopover**: Call AA desk, change dates (free), confirm D-class on new date.

# Appendix C: The 10 Biggest Mistakes to Avoid

### 1. Using a dummy date on the first segment

**Consequence**: Must change it later, triggering repricing if the base fare has increased. One user lost $6,000+.
**Prevention**: Always book segment 1 on a real, flyable date. Fly it within days.

### 2. Plating on Qantas

**Consequence**: Worst service (agents uninformed, 1-3.5 hour calls), worst change handling (refuse free changes, quote AUD 10,065 for $125 change), worst YQ ($334/segment). QF cancelled reservations mid-call without notification.
**Prevention**: Plate on AA (flexibility) or QR via agent (cost). Never QF.

### 3. Booking QF long-haul segments

**Consequence**: EUR 1,240 in surcharges for a single DFW-SYD segment. QF is stingy with D-class. And QF agents make mid-trip changes a nightmare.
**Prevention**: Use AA codeshare on the same physical QF flight (saves ~$1,160). Or re-route via JL transpacific.

### 4. Presenting hub transits as standalone segments to the agent

**Consequence**: CX/QR O&D control blocks standalone D-class to/from their hubs. The agent sees D0.
**Prevention**: For CX/HKG and QR/DOH, present as connected routing: "LAX to Bangkok via Hong Kong."

### 5. No-showing without calling

**Consequence**: All downstream segments automatically cancelled within minutes (coupon-order rule). Irreversible without rebooking.
**Prevention**: Always call the AA RTW desk BEFORE the scheduled departure time if you cannot make a flight.

### 6. Trusting ExpertFlyer as gospel

**Consequence**: 15-30% of "available" segments will fail at booking due to POC restrictions, married segment control, agency restrictions, and carrier capacity limitations.
**Prevention**: Treat EF as an upper bound. Flag hub transits and problematic carriers. Have alternatives ready.

### 7. Not flying the first segment quickly to lock the fare

**Consequence**: Exposure to fare increases. Changes to the first segment trigger repricing at the new (higher) rate. Fare increases of $500-2,000+ between filing periods are documented.
**Prevention**: Book today, fly tomorrow (or as soon as possible).

### 8. Making changes in multiple phone calls instead of bundling

**Consequence**: Paying $125 per call instead of $125 per transaction. Four changes across four calls = $500 instead of $125.
**Prevention**: Plan all routing changes, call once, make all changes in that single transaction.

### 9. Ignoring codeshare codes and YQ optimization

**Consequence**: Same seat, same plane, $500+ difference in surcharges. BA code on LHR-SEA = ~$321. AA code on the same flight = ~$50.
**Prevention**: For every high-YQ segment (BA, QF), check if an AA codeshare exists. Request specific codes during booking. Monitor after schedule changes.

### 10. Not monitoring the ticket after booking

**Consequence**: Schedule changes silently cancel segments, revert codeshare codes, or create impossible connections. CX flights vanished from a QF ticket without notification. YQ optimization undone by silent code reversions.
**Prevention**: Check your itinerary on AA.com weekly. Verify with the ticketing airline after any schedule change notification. Re-request codeshare codes if reverted.

# Quick Reference: The Complete Workflow

```
PLAN (Phase 1)
  1. Choose origin city (Oslo for UK/European travelers)
  2. Design routing: cities, carriers, codeshares
  3. Optimize carrier mix: maximize AY/JL, minimize BA/QF
  4. Decide plating: AA (flexibility) vs QR via agent (cost)
  5. Flag risk segments: hub transits, problematic carriers

VERIFY (Phase 2)
  6. Screen all segments on ExpertFlyer
  7. Categorize: Green / Yellow / Red
  8. Run connection searches for hub transits
  9. Cross-check critical segments via xONEx dummy booking
  10. Prepare alternatives for every Red segment

BOOK (Phase 3)
  11. Call AA RTW desk or contact specialist agent
  12. Feed segments one at a time (segment-by-segment method)
  13. Use connected routing for hub transits
  14. Request specific codeshare codes
  15. Use dummy dates for far-future segments
  16. Confirm all carrier codes before ending the call

LOCK (Phase 4)
  17. FLY THE FIRST SEGMENT IMMEDIATELY
  18. Base fare is now permanently locked
  19. Set up ExpertFlyer alerts for dummy-date and economy segments
  20. Create a date-change calendar for rolling updates

ROLL (Phase 4-5)
  21. As real dates enter booking window, call to change dummy dates (free)
  22. Monitor for D-class openings on economy segments
  23. Bundle routing changes into single calls ($125/transaction)
  24. Exploit schedule changes for free rerouting
  25. Check itinerary weekly; track ticket numbers after every change

FLY (Phase 5)
  26. Complete all segments within 12 months of first departure
  27. Never no-show without calling
  28. Handle disputes by quoting Rule 16
  29. For involuntary downgrades: rebook free or claim EU261/DOT compensation
  30. Skipping the last segment is practically safe (travel carry-on only)
```
